exIQtive works when the people running it treat it as a living system, not a one-time setup. This page covers the key roles that make it work and what success looks like in practice.

## The Organizational Readiness Manager

Every successful exIQtive implementation has someone in the Organizational Readiness Manager (ORM) role. It might be a dedicated person or a leader who takes it on as part of a broader remit. Either way, the ORM makes sure the system is set up correctly, stays current, and is actually used.

### What you do as ORM

- **Build and maintain the catalog.** Define what every position is responsible for, capturing roles and responsibilities clearly so there's no ambiguity about who does what.
- **Designate and develop Architects.** For each responsibility, identify the subject matter expert who will create the institutional knowledge: the videos, articles, and Inspection Protocols that make consistent evaluation possible.
- **Ensure coaching capacity.** Make sure every responsibility has enough certified Coaches to keep up with inspection demand.
- **Report on readiness.** Track and communicate readiness levels to leadership, managers, and employees.

<Tip>
Aim for at least two Coaches per responsibility, so inspections can still happen when one Coach is unavailable.
</Tip>

### What success looks like

The ORM role is working when you consistently hit these targets:

| Indicator | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Positions with clearly defined roles and responsibilities attached | 80% or more |
| Employee readiness across the organization | 90% or more |
| Organizational readiness | 80% or more |

These aren't one-time achievements; they're the ongoing health indicators of a well-run readiness system. If they drop, something in the structure (missing roles, retiring responsibilities, coaching gaps) needs attention.

## The coaching structure that scales

The most effective way to build readiness at scale is a clear chain:

1. **Architects** create the knowledge and certify Coaches.
2. **Coaches** inspect and certify Employees.
3. **Employees** earn badges that prove their competence.

You're not dependent on one person for everything. When an Architect certifies a second Coach for a responsibility, you double your inspection capacity. When that Coach brings more Employees to Proficient, some of them become Coach candidates. The system grows itself, as long as the ORM keeps the pipeline moving.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What happens if we don't have anyone in the ORM role?">
    Without an ORM, exIQtive tends to stall after initial setup. Positions get created but responsibilities don't get attached. Architects get designated but don't create Inspection Protocols. Coaching capacity doesn't grow. The ORM doesn't have to be full-time, but someone needs to own it actively. Even a few hours a week of consistent attention makes a significant difference.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do we get to the success targets quickly?">
    Start with your most critical positions, the ones where poor readiness hurts your business most. Configure those fully first: roles attached, responsibilities defined, an Architect designated, Inspection Protocols created, and at least one Coach certified. Then expand. Trying to do everything at once is the most common reason implementations stall.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What if an Architect leaves the company?">
    This is why at least two Coaches per responsibility matters. If the Architect leaves before you designate a successor, the Coaches can keep inspecting and certifying Employees. But designate a new Architect as soon as possible: no one can update the Inspection Protocols or certify new Coaches until that role is filled again.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
