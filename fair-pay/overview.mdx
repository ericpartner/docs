Fair Base Pay helps you pay each person fairly and explain why. You set a pay range for every position, keep each employee's pay plan with its full history, and see at a glance who is paid fairly and who is not.

<Info>
Fair Base Pay is a separate module. If you don't see **Fair Base Pay** in your side menu, your account doesn't include it yet. Only administrators see Fair Base Pay and its promotion page.
</Info>

## What you can do with Fair Base Pay

- **Set market-based pay ranges.** Create a pay range for each position from your salary surveys. IQ can suggest a minimum and a maximum, and you review them before you save. IQ suggests and a person decides.
- **Keep pay ranges and pay plans over time.** Every change is a new version with a start and end date, so you can see the range or plan in force on any date.
- **Propose and approve pay changes.** A pay change is a proposal that goes through approval before it takes effect. Nothing changes silently.
- **See fair pay for each employee.** Fair Base Pay shows what fair pay is for an employee and how it compares with what the employee is paid today. The calculation uses a proprietary formula.

## The Fair Base Pay menu

The Fair Base Pay section has six items.

| Menu item | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| **Pay Plans** | Every employee with their current pay plan, any proposed change, and their fair pay status. |
| **Positions** | Each position with the pay range it subscribes to. |
| **Pay Ranges** | Your pay ranges, their versions and the positions that use them. |
| **Job Families** | The disciplines and seniority levels you assign to positions. See [Job families](/fair-pay/job-families). |
| **Job Categories** | The groups of positions with work of equal value, which you compare pay across. See [Job categories](/fair-pay/job-categories). |
| **Market Data** | The salary survey files you upload as evidence for your pay ranges. |

## How the pieces fit together

<Steps>
  <Step title="Upload market data">
    Add the salary surveys and benchmarking reports you trust to **Market Data**. They give your pay ranges a market basis.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create pay ranges">
    Set a minimum and a maximum for each pay range, in the country and currency it applies to. Set up your [operating countries and currencies](/account/operating-countries-and-currencies) first.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Subscribe positions to pay ranges">
    Each [position](/organization/positions) subscribes to a pay range, so every employee in that position is compared with the right range. See [Positions and pay ranges](/fair-pay/position-subscriptions).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Keep a pay plan for each employee">
    An employee's pay plan is a series of dated versions. When pay should change, you propose a new version and send it for approval.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review fair pay">
    Use **Pay Plans** to see who is paid fairly, who is out of range and which proposals are waiting.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Pay ranges and versions

A pay range is never overwritten. When you change it, you create a new version that starts on a date you choose. You can look at your pay ranges as of any date to see the version that was in force then. If you made a mistake in a version, you can correct it in place. See [Pay ranges and versions](/fair-pay/pay-ranges).

## Pay plans and approvals

Each version of a pay plan has a status:

- **Proposed** while it waits for approval.
- **In force** once it is approved and active.
- **Terminated** when it ends.
- **Voided** when it is cancelled before it takes effect.

From the dates, Fair Pay also shows whether a version is lapsed, current or future, so you can read an employee's pay history on a timeline.

## Who can use Fair Base Pay

Owners, co-owners, and administrators can use Fair Base Pay and make changes in it. Administrators don't need a separate permission for it. Employees don't see Fair Base Pay. See [permission levels](/account/users-and-permissions).
