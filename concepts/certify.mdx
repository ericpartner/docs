Certify is the second step of the exIQtive method. Once responsibilities are [clear](/concepts/clarify), Certify proves that each employee is **ready** to perform them, and turns that proof into a number you can manage.

## Why certify

Most organizations train on the job. An expert coaches a new hire through their responsibilities, and the knowledge passes by word of mouth. That knowledge is **tribal**: it lives in people's heads, so it's hard to scale and hard to check.

The goal is an organization where most employees are competent, a few are experts, and everyone gets there fast. Certification creates accountability on both sides: the employee knows what readiness requires, and the expert's results are multiplied across the people they certify.

## Certify readiness, not training

Attending training doesn't prove anything. Readiness does. An employee is ready when they:

1. **Know** what to do (knowledge)
2. **Can do it** (ability)
3. **Do it** (behavior)

exIQtive certifies all three, one responsibility at a time.

## One badge per responsibility

Every responsibility an employee holds has its own badge, and each badge sits on one of five levels:

| Badge | Readiness | What it certifies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Novice | 0% | Can't do it yet |
| Apprentice | 50% | Can do it, supervised |
| Proficient | 100% | Can do it, unsupervised |
| Coach | 125% | Trains and certifies employees |
| Architect | 150% | Builds institutional knowledge and certifies Coaches |

The levels fall into three zones: **Developing** (Novice and Apprentice), **Competent** (Proficient), and **Expert** (Coach and Architect). See [The badge ladder](/user-guide/readiness/badge-ladder) for how badges are earned and what keeps them valid.

## Inspection, not evaluation

An evaluation is subjective: it relies on one person's view, drawn from tribal knowledge. An **inspection** is objective: it checks the employee against institutional knowledge that everyone can see.

Objective certification takes three steps:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Capture knowledge">
    An Architect captures how the responsibility is performed, in short videos, articles, and quizzes.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define certification criteria">
    The Architect turns that knowledge into checkpoints, which together form the Inspection Protocol.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Certify">
    A Coach inspects the employee against the checkpoints and issues the badge.
  </Step>
</Steps>

See [Checkpoints](/checkpoints/overview) and [Inspecting a responsibility](/coaching/inspecting-a-responsibility).

## Measure readiness

Because every badge has a percentage, readiness is simple math: you can't manage what you don't measure.

An employee's readiness is the average of their responsibilities' badge levels. For example, an employee with an Apprentice (50%), a Proficient (100%), and a Coach (125%) badge on three equally important responsibilities has a readiness of (50 + 100 + 125) ÷ 3 = **92%**.

<Note>
  exIQtive weights each responsibility by its importance, so the percentage you see can differ from a simple average. See [How readiness is calculated](/user-guide/readiness/how-readiness-is-calculated).
</Note>

When readiness is visible to everyone, it drives accountability: what's measured gets improved. See [Communicating readiness to your employees](/readiness/communicating-readiness).

### Ready today, growing for tomorrow

Readiness covers more than the job someone does today. An employee's readiness for their **primary position** shows how ready they are now. Their readiness for a **next position** they're preparing for shows how far along they are on that path. Positions in a [job family](/organization/job-families) form a ladder, such as Staff Accountant, Senior Accountant, Controller, and CFO. See [Employee/position assignments](/organization/employee-position-assignments).

### What reduces readiness

Three things can lower an employee's readiness:

- **Changed responsibilities:** the responsibilities for a position change.
- **Badge expiration:** a badge reaches the end of its grace period or its responsibility is retired.
- **Badge removal:** an administrator removes a badge.

An expired or removed badge counts as Novice (0%) until the employee is inspected again.
