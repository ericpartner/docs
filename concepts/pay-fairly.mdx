Pay fairly is the third step of the exIQtive method. Once employees are [clear](/concepts/clarify) on their responsibilities and [certified](/concepts/certify) as ready, Pay fairly makes sure that base pay and bonus reflect it. It has two parts: **Fair Base Pay** and **Fair Bonus**.

<Note>
  **Fair Base Pay** is available now. **Fair Bonuses** is <Badge>Coming soon</Badge>. See the [Fair Base Pay overview](/fair-pay/overview) for what you can do today.
</Note>

## Fair Base Pay <Badge>Available</Badge>

Fair Base Pay answers one question for each employee: is this person paid fairly for the job they do?

- You set a **pay range** for every position, with a minimum and a maximum.
- You keep each employee's **pay plan** with its full history.
- You see at a glance who is paid fairly and who isn't, and you can explain why.

Because pay ranges attach to positions, and positions connect to [job families](/organization/job-families) and [job categories](/organization/job-categories), the same structure that clarifies the work also sets what it pays.

## Fair Bonus <Badge>Coming soon</Badge>

Fair Bonus isn't available yet. This section explains how it will work.

Fair Bonus ties a bonus to two things: how the company performs, and how ready the employee is. Four numbers give you the result:

| Step | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Potential** | Bonus rate times base salary | 30% × \$10,000 = \$3,000 |
| **Eligibility** | 1 if the employee meets the conditions, 0 if not | 90+ days in role and EBITDA above 0 gives 1 |
| **Multiplier** | Company score times readiness times eligibility | 0.8 × 75% × 1 = 0.6 |
| **Fair bonus** | Potential times multiplier | \$3,000 × 0.6 = \$1,800 |

The example shows how the calculation works. Your own rates, conditions, and scores come from your account settings.

Readiness is part of the multiplier, so a certified employee earns more of their bonus potential than one who isn't ready yet. See [How readiness is calculated](/user-guide/readiness/how-readiness-is-calculated).
