Readiness is the heart of exIQtive. This page explains what readiness means, how it's expressed as a percentage and a badge, and how Coaches inspect and certify it.

## Employee readiness

Readiness is the state where an employee can perform their responsibilities **independently, consistently, and to the required standard**, without supervision and without guesswork. It's not about completing training; it's about whether the employee can actually do the work when it counts.

In practice, readiness covers three things:

1. **Clarity**: the employee knows exactly what's expected
2. **Capability**: they have the skills and knowledge to deliver
3. **Consistency**: they can do it reliably, not just once

## Readiness level

Every employee has a Readiness level for each responsibility they hold, expressed as a percentage from 0% to 150%. The higher the percentage, the more mastery they've demonstrated:

| Zone | Range | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Developing** | 0%–50% | Still learning, needs support |
| **Competent** | 51%–100% | Can do the work, with or without supervision depending on badge level |
| **Expert** | 101%–150% | Can coach others or define the standard for the work |

These zones reflect meaningfully different levels of contribution to your organization. See [How readiness is calculated](/user-guide/readiness/how-readiness-is-calculated) for how individual responsibility levels roll up into an overall score.

## Readiness badge

A Readiness Badge is the formal certification that an employee is ready to perform a specific responsibility at a defined level. A Coach (or an Architect acting as one) issues it after an inspection. It's the evidence that readiness is real, not assumed.

There are five badge levels, each at a specific point on the Readiness scale:

| Badge | Readiness | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Novice | 0% | No badge yet: the employee hasn't been certified for this responsibility |
| Apprentice | 50% | Can perform the responsibility, but still needs supervision |
| Proficient | 100% | Can perform independently and consistently: the standard expectation |
| Coach | 125% | Can inspect and certify others on this responsibility |
| Architect | 150% | Can define the standard, create the knowledge base, and train Coaches |

Each employee holds one badge per responsibility at a time. Earning a higher badge replaces the previous one, and your Readiness level updates immediately.

<Note>
Only a **Current** badge contributes its Readiness percentage to your score. An **Expired** or **Removed** badge counts as 0% (Novice). See [The badge ladder](/user-guide/readiness/badge-ladder) for the full explanation of badge statuses.
</Note>

## Awarding vs giving a badge

A Coach can issue a badge in two ways, depending on whether the responsibility has an Inspection Protocol:

- **Awarding**: the Coach uses the Inspection Protocol to formally inspect the employee, checkpoint by checkpoint. This is the preferred approach: objective, repeatable, and fair.
- **Giving**: the Coach uses their experience and judgment to issue the badge directly. This is only available when no Inspection Protocol exists for the responsibility.

## Inspection Protocol

An Inspection Protocol is the structured set of criteria a Coach uses to evaluate whether an employee is ready to perform a responsibility. It breaks the responsibility into observable actions, knowledge requirements, and behavioral standards, making the assessment objective rather than a matter of opinion.

The Inspection Protocol is a collection of checkpoints. The Architect for the responsibility creates and maintains it; keeping it current is one of the Architect's core duties.

## Inspection checkpoint

A checkpoint is one item in an Inspection Protocol. Each checkpoint targets a specific readiness level and comes with clear criteria and instructions for evaluating it.

During an inspection, a Coach marks each checkpoint as **Passed**, **Failed**, or **Not Applicable (N/A)**. N/A checkpoints are treated as satisfied; they don't block a badge when the criterion genuinely doesn't apply.

Checkpoints can also include short videos and quizzes as microlearning, so employees have what they need to prepare before requesting an inspection.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between a Readiness level and a badge?">
    Your Readiness level is a percentage that reflects how well you've demonstrated competence. A badge is the certification for one specific responsibility at one specific level. Badges drive the percentage: earning a new badge for a responsibility immediately updates your Readiness for that responsibility.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why does the scale go to 150% instead of stopping at 100%?">
    Doing your job well (Proficient, 100%) is different from being able to train others to do it (Coach, 125%) or defining how it should be done across the organization (Architect, 150%). The extra range recognizes and rewards the employees who multiply your organization's capability, not just their own.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What's the difference between awarding and giving a badge?">
    Awarding is the formal path: the Coach works through the Inspection Protocol checkpoint by checkpoint. Giving is the fallback when no protocol exists and relies on the Coach's judgment. Both result in a badge, but awarding is preferred because it produces a consistent, auditable result that doesn't depend on any one Coach's style.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Who creates the Inspection Protocol?">
    The Architect for the responsibility. Creating and maintaining the Inspection Protocol, along with the videos, knowledge articles, and quizzes that support it, is one of an Architect's core duties. IQ, exIQtive's AI assistant, can suggest checkpoints to help the Architect build and refine the protocol. Without a protocol, Coaches can only give badges, not award them.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a checkpoint be skipped?">
    Yes. If a checkpoint genuinely doesn't apply to the employee or situation, the Coach can mark it Not Applicable. N/A checkpoints are treated as satisfied and don't prevent the employee from earning the badge.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
