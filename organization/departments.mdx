title: "Departments"
description: "Group positions into a department tree to navigate your organization and view readiness by team."
---

Departments let you group Positions into a hierarchy that mirrors your organization's structure. They help you organize and navigate your workforce; they don't change how Responsibilities are assigned or how Readiness is calculated.

<Note>
**The one exception is Fair Pay.** If a Position doesn't have its own Performance Scorecard or Bonus Eligibility Criteria, it inherits them from its Department. See [Fair Pay](/fair-reward/fair-pay) for how that works.
</Note>

## Where to find departments

Go to **Account → Organization → Departments**. The view shows your organization as a tree. Each node is a Department, and Positions (with the employees who hold them) sit inside Departments.

## What departments are for

- **Navigation** — ORMs and Co-Owners can browse the organization by team, division, or unit instead of scrolling a flat list of Positions.
- **Reporting** — Readiness can be viewed per Department, showing how ready a specific team or division is.

An employee's Responsibilities come from the Position → Role → Responsibility structure, not from their Department. See [Key organizational concepts](/concepts/organization).

## View department readiness

Every Department row in the tree shows its own Readiness, so you don't need a separate report.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the tree">
    Go to **Account → Organization → Departments**. The tree view opens by default.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Read the Readiness column">
    Each row shows a **Readiness** bar and percentage.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drill in">
    Click the Readiness bar (or anywhere on the row, or either position count) to see that Department's Positions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

The percentage in the tree is always the **subtree** figure: the pooled average across that Department and every Department nested beneath it, at any depth. When you're scanning the whole organization, the rollup is what matters.

Once you drill in, you can toggle between:

- **This Department only** — just the Positions directly in this Department
- **Including sub-departments** — the same subtree rollup shown in the tree

A Department with no active Positions in scope shows **0%**. A retired Department also shows **0%**, because retiring a Department releases all its Positions.

## The department tree

Departments are nested. A Department can contain Positions (with their assigned employees), sub-departments, or both, with no limit on depth. A typical structure looks like:

**Company → Division → Team → Positions**

A Position can belong to only one Department at a time. Positions not placed in any Department appear as unassigned.

## Add a new department

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open Departments">
    Go to **Account → Organization → Departments**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the department">
    Click **+ Add Department** and enter the Department name.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a parent">
    Select a parent Department, or leave it at the top level to create a root Department.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**. The Department appears in the tree immediately, ready to receive Positions.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Department actions

Most actions are in the three-dot menu (⋯) on the right side of the Department row.

### Rename

Updates the Department name everywhere it appears. No Positions or assignments are affected.

### Create a sub-department

There's no dedicated "Add Sub-department" action in the row menu. To nest a new Department, click **+ Add Department** in the header and choose the existing Department as the parent.

### Move

Changes the Department's parent. All Positions and sub-departments inside it move with it.

<Warning>
If the move would change the Performance Scorecard or Bonus Eligibility Criteria a Position inherits, you'll see a warning before the move completes. See [Fair Pay](/fair-reward/fair-pay).
</Warning>

### Archive

Removes the Department from the active tree. Positions inside it become unassigned. Archive a Department only when it no longer represents an active part of the organization.

<Warning>
If Positions beneath the Department rely on its Fair Pay configuration (Scorecard or Bonus Eligibility Criteria), you'll see a warning before archiving completes.
</Warning>

### Delete

Permanently deletes the Department. Only available when it's empty (no Positions and no sub-departments). This can't be undone.

## Add a position to a department

<Steps>
  <Step title="Find the department">
    Find the Department in the tree.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add the position">
    Click **+ Add Position** (or drag an existing Position into the Department), then search for and select the Position.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm">
    Confirm. The Position now appears under that Department.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Move a position between departments

Drag the Position row from its current Department and drop it into the target Department. Its assignments, Roles, and Responsibilities aren't affected. Its Fair Pay resolution (Performance Scorecard and Bonus Eligibility Criteria) may change if the Position doesn't have its own override. See [Fair Pay](/fair-reward/fair-pay).

## Remove a position from a department

Open the Position's row menu inside the Department and select **Remove from Department**. The Position becomes unassigned and appears in the unassigned Positions list.

## Rules and constraints

- A Position can belong to only one Department at a time.
- Departments can be nested to any depth.
- You can delete a Department only when it's empty.
- Archiving a Department leaves its Positions unassigned. They don't move to the parent Department automatically.
- Department membership doesn't affect Responsibility assignment or Readiness calculation. Fair Pay (Performance Scorecard, Bonus Eligibility Criteria) is the exception.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does moving a Position to a different Department change the employee's Responsibilities?">
    No. Responsibilities come from the Position → Role → Responsibility structure, not from the Department.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What happens to an employee's Readiness data if their Position moves to another Department?">
    Nothing changes. Readiness is calculated from badges and Responsibilities, not from Department membership.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How can I see the readiness of a Department?">
    Go to **Account → Organization → Departments**. Every Department row shows a **Readiness** bar and percentage, rolled up across that Department and everything beneath it. Click the bar or row to drill in and toggle between the Department's own Positions and the full rollup. See [View department readiness](#view-department-readiness).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What does it mean for a Position to be unassigned?">
    The Position exists but isn't placed in any Department. It still works normally (employees can be assigned and Responsibilities flow through it), but it doesn't appear in any Department's branch of the tree. For Fair Pay, an unassigned Position has no Department to inherit a Scorecard or Bonus Eligibility Criteria from. See [Fair Pay](/fair-reward/fair-pay).
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a Department contain both Positions and sub-departments?">
    Yes, in any mix.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does moving or archiving a Department affect Fair Pay?">
    Yes. This is the one place Department placement has a functional effect. A Position without its own Performance Scorecard or Bonus Eligibility Criteria inherits its Department's. Moving or archiving a Department that Positions rely on for that shows a warning before you proceed. See [Fair Pay](/fair-reward/fair-pay).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
