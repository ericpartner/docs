Your organization in exIQtive is the structure of your employees, the positions they hold, the departments those positions sit in, and the work each position is responsible for. It's the foundation everything else builds on: Readiness, badges, inspections, and fair pay all flow from how your organization is set up.

## The three building blocks

Every position in your organization is described with three things.

### Position

A position is a job in your organization, such as *Production Manager*, *Shift Supervisor*, or *Customer Support Lead*. When you assign an employee to a position, they automatically receive everything the position includes.

### Role

A role is a reusable group of responsibilities, such as *Scheduler*, *Budget Owner*, or *Recruiter*. You can attach several roles to one position, and the same role can appear in many positions, so you only define it once.

### Responsibility

A responsibility is a specific area of work someone must be able to perform, such as *Schedule Staff*, *Define Jobs*, or *Hire Candidates*. Responsibilities are what exIQtive inspects and certifies against; they're the unit of readiness.

## How it connects

The chain is simple: **Position → Role → Responsibility → Employee**.

A responsibility can also be attached directly to a position, skipping the role. Either way, when you assign an employee to a position, they automatically receive every responsibility attached to it. You don't assign anything individually; the structure does the work for you.

## Departments

A department groups positions into a hierarchy that mirrors how your company is structured, such as _Company → Division → Team_. Departments can be nested to any depth, and each position belongs to one department at a time.

Departments are for navigating your organization and viewing Readiness by team. They sit alongside the chain above, not inside it: an employee's roles and responsibilities come from their position, never from their department.

<Note>
  The one exception is Fair Pay. A position without its own Performance Scorecard or Bonus Eligibility Criteria inherits them from its department. See [Fair Pay](/fair-reward/fair-pay).
</Note>

## A real-life example

Your organization is **Café Delight**, with three positions: *Barista*, *Shift Supervisor*, and *Store Manager*.

- The **Barista** position includes the *Customer Service* role, which has three responsibilities: Take Orders, Prepare Drinks, and Clean Equipment. When you assign Anna to Barista, she automatically gets all three.
- The **Shift Supervisor** position includes both the *Customer Service* role and the *Leadership* role. When you assign Leo to Shift Supervisor, he gets Anna's three responsibilities plus the leadership ones, like Manage Inventory.

Same roles, defined once, reused across both positions.

To see readiness by team, you group the three positions into a **Café Delight → Front of House** department. Anna and Leo still receive exactly the same responsibilities; the department only organizes them for navigation and reporting.

## Common questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Do I have to set up roles, or can I attach responsibilities directly to positions?">
    You can do either. A responsibility can reach a position through a role, or you can attach it directly to the position. Use a role when a group of responsibilities is shared by several positions, so you define it once and update it in one place. Attach directly when a responsibility belongs to only one position and doesn't need a role around it.

    Employees assigned to the position receive the responsibility either way. If the same responsibility ends up on a position both directly and through a role, the direct attachment is marked **Redundant**, and you can remove it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="If I change a role, does it update everywhere?">
    Yes. Roles are shared across positions. Add or remove a responsibility from a role and every position that includes it picks up the change automatically, along with every employee assigned to those positions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do departments change which responsibilities an employee has?">
    No. Responsibilities flow only through Position → Role → Responsibility. Departments organize positions for navigation and for viewing Readiness by team. The one exception is Fair Pay, where a position without its own Scorecard or Bonus Eligibility Criteria inherits them from its department.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can a position have multiple roles?">
    Yes, and most do. A Shift Supervisor might have a Customer Service role and a Leadership role; employees in that position get the responsibilities from both.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
